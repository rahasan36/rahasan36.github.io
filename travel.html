Index/Travel

Travel diaries.

A few notes and photographs from the places we have traveled together. My work looks at cities from a planetary scale, and these trips are where I slow down and notice the small things on the ground.

2026

3 trips
July 2026 / 48°42′N · 113°43′W / Glacier National Park, Montana

Glacier National Park

Many Glacier has a quiet, old-fashioned beauty: historic lodges, still lakes, and peaks rising right behind them. That feeling stayed with us all the way along Going-to-the-Sun Road. We had not planned for the smoke. Haze from Canadian wildfires softened every ridgeline into pale layers, and on some days the far shore of Lake McDonald disappeared completely. When the air cleared, even for an hour, the views felt like a gift.

May 2026 / 41°53′N · 87°38′W / Chicago, Illinois

A few days in Chicago

This album is mostly food, and that is an honest record of the trip. We ate our way across the city: tempura, lamb over coals, a steak still sizzling when it arrived, sushi, and stew in a bread bowl. The architecture can wait for next time.

March 2026 / 36°03′N · 112°08′W / Arizona & Utah

The Colorado Plateau loop

Four stops on one long loop: the Grand Canyon, Zion, the hoodoos of Bryce, and Horseshoe Bend. March was a kind month to go, with few crowds, cold mornings, and clear light that lasted all day. Somewhere along the canyon rim, the size of it stopped being a number and became something we could feel.

October 2025 / 42°22′N · 71°04′W / Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine & Massachusetts

Fall in New England

We went for the fall colors and found them at their peak. Whole hillsides turned at once, and lakes were ringed in orange. The Kancamagus Highway through the White Mountains became one long, slow drive through red and gold. A white church in a small Vermont village looked almost too perfect to be real. Boston, at the end, felt like coming back indoors.

July 2025 / 44°26′N · 110°35′W / Wyoming & Montana

Yellowstone and Grand Teton

We started in Yellowstone, with the Lower Falls pouring into its canyon and Grand Prismatic spread out in rings of color. Then we drove south to the Tetons, where the mountains rise straight out of the sagebrush with nothing in front of them. The Moulton barn on Mormon Row is photographed a thousand times a day, and it was still worth stopping for.

June 2025 / 39°44′N · 104°59′W / Colorado

Through Colorado

June is when the high country has only just opened. Alpine lakes still held snow on their far shores, Sprague Lake sat calm beneath the peaks of Rocky Mountain National Park, and the Maroon Bells rose over their lake streaked with the last of the snow. The Black Canyon of the Gunnison was narrower and darker than any photograph had prepared us for.

1 trip
December 2023 / 37°45′N · 119°36′W / California & Nevada

California, Nevada, and everything between

We rode the cable cars in San Francisco, then drove to Yosemite, where winter light made Half Dome and El Capitan look sharp and close. Among the sequoias we stood beneath General Sherman, the largest tree on Earth by volume. Knowing the number did not prepare us for how small we felt under it. Nevada closed out the trip.

October 2022 / 34°31′N · 93°03′W / Arkansas

Hot Springs National Park

Hot Springs is a national park wrapped around a working town. You can step off Bathhouse Row and straight onto forest trails. In early October the Ouachita Mountains were just beginning to turn, and from the tower on the mountain the whole town lay quietly below us.

2019

3 trips
Thanksgiving 2019 / 49°53′N · 119°30′W / Kelowna, British Columbia

Thanksgiving in Kelowna

We spent Thanksgiving on Okanagan Lake, with Kelowna stretched along the far shore and the hills rising behind it. The trees were bare, evenings came early, and the town was lit up by five. It was the quiet side of a summer place, and that was exactly what we wanted.

June 2019 / 44°26′N · 110°35′W / Wyoming

Yellowstone and the Tetons, first time

Our first trip out west. Bison grazed at the roadside, the falls thundered in the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone, and the Tetons appeared through cloud over the Snake River. In a way, most of our trips since have been about coming back to this feeling.

June 2019 / 44°35′N · 104°43′W / Wyoming & South Dakota

Devils Tower, Rushmore, and the Badlands

The same June trip carried us east. Devils Tower stood alone above the grassland, then came Mount Rushmore, and then the Badlands, which looked less like a finished landscape than something still being shaped.
