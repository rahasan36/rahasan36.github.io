I grew up sketching cities on tracing paper in Khulna and now spend my days writing the code that helps other people analyze them. My research traces how urban expansion and road networks remake landscapes, and what that change means for the species and people sharing those landscapes.

At Esri, I design geoprocessing and raster analytics tools that ship inside ArcGIS Pro. In parallel, I publish on global biodiversity loss, protected-area isolation, and infrastructure exposure to climate hazards. The two halves feed each other. The science tells me which tools are missing. Building tools keeps the science honest about what computation actually permits.

Background

Before joining Esri in 2024, I completed a PhD in Geography at Texas A&M, advised by Dr. Burak Güneralp. My dissertation produced one of the first global-scale road network growth models. Part of the work was highlighted in AGU's Eos magazine. Earlier, I held two master's degrees from Iowa State, one in Community and Regional Planning and one in Transportation, with a graduate certificate in GIS along the way. My undergraduate degree in Urban and Rural Planning is from Khulna University. The planning identity runs underneath everything I do.

Outside work

I am Bangladeshi, and live in the United States. My wife and I travel whenever we can. I write in Bengali and English, mostly essays that sit somewhere between memoir and field notes.
