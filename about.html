I grew up sketching cities on tracing paper in Khulna and now spend my days writing the code that helps other people analyze them. My research traces how urban expansion and road networks remake landscapes, and what that change means for the species and people sharing those landscapes.

At Esri I work in geospatial R&D, applying spatial analysis to questions about urban expansion and infrastructure. In parallel, I publish on global biodiversity loss, protected-area isolation, and infrastructure exposure to climate hazards. The two halves feed each other — the applied work keeps the academic questions grounded, and the academic work keeps the applied work asking the right things.

Methodologically my work sits at the convergence of machine learning, statistical modeling, and large-scale spatial analysis. Across projects I lean on hedonic and regression models, machine-learning approaches to land-cover classification, statistical and AI-based flood susceptibility modeling, and large process models for urban–transportation coevolution. The common thread is a preference for models that take spatial structure seriously, communicate uncertainty honestly, and remain interpretable.

Recent collaborators include Burak Güneralp and Lee A. Fitzgerald at Texas A&M, the Oak Ridge National Laboratory Human Geography Group, and Karen Seto and Michael Goodchild on earlier projects. I co-convene the AGU Urban Area and Global Change session in 2026, and earlier work fed into IPBES and IPCC contributing-author chapters.

Background

Before joining Esri in 2024 I completed a PhD in Geography at Texas A&M, advised by Burak Güneralp. My dissertation produced what I believe is still the only global-scale road network growth model, work that AGU's Eos magazine featured. Earlier I held two master's degrees from Iowa State, one in Community and Regional Planning and one in Transportation, with a graduate certificate in GIS along the way. My undergraduate degree in Urban and Rural Planning is from Khulna University. The planning identity runs underneath everything I do.

Outside work

I am Bangladeshi, married, and live in the United States. My wife and I travel whenever we can. I write in Bengali and English, mostly essays that sit somewhere between memoir and field notes.
