Index/Research

Research.

Seven threads, one question: how do cities reshape the planet? My work answers it through the lenses of land change, infrastructure, biodiversity, human–environment interactions, health, the spatial models that hold them together, and the geospatial craft underneath all of it.

Theme I

Urban land change & expansion

Long-term, high-resolution maps of where urban land has grown remain scarce. I reconstruct three decades of urban expansion from satellite records and project it forward under several development futures, keeping regional differences visible rather than folding them into one global curve.

  • Global futures. Divergent urban boundaries and high spatial uncertainty in global urban land projections. In preparation
  • Gulf coast. Settlement on the US Gulf coast moved while the hurricane corridor stayed in place. In preparation
  • Bangladesh. Hidden urban exposure around protected areas and species habitats, at iScience. Under review
  • Protected areas. Urban growth around the world's protected areas, 1992 to 2020, at Nature Sustainability. Revision submitted

Theme II

Road network growth modeling

My doctoral work produced one of the first global-scale road network growth models, calibrated against historical road expansion and projected under several development scenarios. AGU's Eos magazine featured the work.

  • Hierarchical growth. A location-based model that forecasts road growth by road class, at Nature Communications. In press
  • Siting and hazard. How infrastructure siting decisions shape future hazard exposure. In preparation
  • Healthcare access. Access in India and Bangladesh, with road and urban growth modeled as one coupled process. In preparation

Theme III

Biodiversity & protected areas

Protected areas are the main instrument the world has for holding onto habitat. This thread asks how well that instrument works while cities and roads grow around it. The recurring finding is that the pressure now sits at the edge: reserves are less often built over than surrounded and cut off.

  • Roads and cities together. Coupled urban and road growth reveals underestimated tropical biodiversity loss in Brazil, Indonesia, and Nigeria, with Burak Güneralp and Lee A. Fitzgerald, at Nature Sustainability. Under review
  • Network isolation. How urban growth isolated the global protected-area network between 1992 and 2020, with Güneralp, at Nature Sustainability. Revision submitted
  • Tropical species. The geography of threatened species exposure to urban growth across the tropics. In preparation
  • Bangladesh. Hidden urban exposure around protected areas and species habitats, at iScience. Under review

Theme IV

Human–environment interactions

This theme ties the previous three together. Cities, roads, and infrastructure are coupled with ecosystems, and I ask where that coupling produces conflict (habitat loss, hazard exposure, lost services) and where it produces opportunity (compact development, accessibility, conservation gains).

  • Gulf ocean heat. Rapid hurricane intensification in the Gulf of Mexico and the daily upper-ocean heat beneath the storm track. In preparation
  • Gulf coast settlement. How settlement on the US Gulf coast moved while the hurricane corridor stayed in place, 1985 to 2024. In preparation
  • Nigeria. Built environment and socioeconomic exposure to flooding. In preparation
  • Global flood co-exposure. River and coastal flood risk to road infrastructure worldwide. In preparation
  • Tropical biodiversity. Coupled urban and road growth and the biodiversity loss it implies. Under review

Theme V

Health GIS

A parallel thread applies spatial methods to public health, from disease spread to access to care.

  • COVID-19. Mapping the spread of the pandemic across multiple geographies.
  • Homelessness research. How geospatial analyses are used in studies of homeless populations.
  • Pandemic decisions. Systematic reviews of the role of GIS in pandemic decision-making.
  • GeoAI and resource allocation. A decision science framework and research agenda, with M Mahbub Hossain. In preparation
  • Healthcare access to 2050. Travel time to primary, secondary, and tertiary care in India and Bangladesh, from a coupled urban and road growth model. In preparation

Theme VI

Spatial modeling

How we model spatial processes comes up in every theme above. This thread brings together machine learning, statistical, and process-based approaches.

  • Statistical. Hedonic and regression models for housing and accessibility.
  • Machine learning. Supervised and unsupervised land-cover classification, and statistical and AI-based flood susceptibility modeling.
  • Process models. Large-scale models of urban and transportation coevolution, including the road network growth model at Nature Communications. In press
  • Uncertainty. Critical facility access in Bangladesh with flood-susceptibility uncertainty carried through to network failure, rather than a single map. In preparation
  • Projection uncertainty. Divergent urban boundaries and high spatial uncertainty in global urban land projections. In preparation

Theme VII

GIS & geospatial data

Every result on this page rests on geospatial decisions that rarely reach the abstract. This thread treats that craft as the subject rather than the plumbing.

  • Product choice. Which dataset to trust, and what a 300-meter pixel actually means on the ground.
  • Accounting. Partitioning overlapping buffers so no hectare is counted twice.
  • Robustness. Testing whether an answer survives a change of threshold.
  • In practice. Cross-dataset validation and buffer partitioning for the protected-area study Revision submitted, and the susceptibility modeling and uncertainty handling behind the flood-access study. In preparation
